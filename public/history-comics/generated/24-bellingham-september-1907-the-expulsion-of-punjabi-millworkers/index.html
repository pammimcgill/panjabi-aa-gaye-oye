Bellingham, September 1907: the expulsion of Punjabi millworkers

How a mob forced Punjabi millworkers out of Bellingham in 1907

Come along to Bellingham, Washington, in the summer of 1907, when Punjabi men worked hard in the lumber mills but faced growing anger from some of their neighbors. This is a true and difficult story about what happened to them, and how the city remembers it today.

1

Mills, Workers, and Rising Anger

By 1907, about 250 Punjabi workers from South Asia had found jobs in Bellingham's lumber and shingle mills. Local newspapers and a group called the Asiatic Exclusion League spread rumors and demanded that no South Asians be employed in the city after Labor Day.

2

The Mob Gathers

On the evening of September 4, 1907, about five hundred white working men gathered in Bellingham with the goal of scaring the South Asian mill workers out of town. The crowd then moved through the streets toward the mills.

3

Pulled From Mills and Homes

The rioters broke into the mills and pulled South Asian workers away from their jobs. They also entered the bunkhouses where the men lived, destroying and stealing their belongings.

4

A Night in the Basement

That night, Punjabi workers were driven to the city limits or taken to the jail in the basement of City Hall. Accounts differ on exact numbers, but well over a hundred men were held there, with police saying it was for their own safety until they agreed to leave town.

5

Scattering South and North

Within days, nearly all the South Asian workers had left Bellingham. Some traveled south toward Seattle and other coastal towns, while others walked back across the border into Canada, arriving in Vancouver just before a similar riot broke out there three days later.

6

Remembering Bellingham Today

In 2018, Bellingham dedicated the Arch of Healing and Reconciliation, a granite monument honoring immigrants, including the Punjabi workers expelled in 1907. The city continues to hold commemorations and share this history with new generations.
