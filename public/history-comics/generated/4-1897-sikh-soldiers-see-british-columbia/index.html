1897: Sikh soldiers see British Columbia

Punjabi soldiers glimpse British Columbia during Queen Victoria's Jubilee year

A child flipping through an old photo album might wonder who these soldiers in turbans were, so far from home. Let's follow the story, page by page, just as historians have pieced it together.

1

A Queen's Diamond Jubilee

In 1897, Queen Victoria celebrated sixty years as Queen with a huge Diamond Jubilee. Soldiers from across the British Empire, including Sikh soldiers, gathered in London for the celebrations.

2

Soldiers bound for Canada

As part of the same jubilee celebrations, Sikh regiments that had marched in London's procession also traveled on to Canada. This was part of an empire-wide tour, not a plan to settle.

3

Landing at Vancouver

In May 1897, a group of cavalry officers, including a senior officer named Kesur Singh, sailed from Hong Kong to Vancouver aboard the ship Empress of India. He is remembered as the first Sikh person to reach Canada.

4

Which way were they going?

Historians don't fully agree on this part of the story. Some records say the soldiers were heading toward the Jubilee in London, while others say they passed through Canada afterward, on their way home.

5

A second visit, five years later

In 1902, another group of Punjabi soldiers came to British Columbia, this time for the coronation of King Edward VII. They arrived in Victoria aboard the Empress of Japan, led by an officer named Kadir Khan Bahadur. Some in the Sikh community later remembered that the mountains and valleys reminded the soldiers of Punjab, though this memory was passed down rather than written at the time.

6

Not settlers, just yet

These soldiers were not immigrants — they were on duty for the empire. Real Punjabi settlement in British Columbia began later, starting in 1904, but the stories these soldiers carried home may have helped draw future travelers across the Pacific.
