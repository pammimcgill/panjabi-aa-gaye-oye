Why They Left

Land, Army and Empire in Panjab · 1880s–1900s

“Let's go back in time and see why so many Punjabi families began looking for a new life — it's a story about land, the army, and a growing empire.”

1

Land and debt

When harvests failed, families borrowed money — and if they couldn't repay it, moneylenders could take their land.

2

New land through canals

The British opened new irrigated farmland in western Panjab. Over a million Punjabi families moved there hoping for a fresh start.

Canal Colony — New Land, New Opportunities.
3

Called to serve

British recruiters called Punjabi men natural soldiers and signed up growing numbers from Panjab's villages. Army pay became one of the few steady incomes for families.

4

A first glimpse of the Pacific

In 1897, Sikh soldiers sailing to London for Queen Victoria's Jubilee passed through Vancouver. Word of the land they saw traveled home.

Vancouver, 1897
5

Three pressures, one village

Debt, new land, and army life were all changing who could stay — and who started looking farther from home.

6

The journey ahead

By the early 1900s, these same pressures would send the first Punjabi immigrants across the Pacific for good.
