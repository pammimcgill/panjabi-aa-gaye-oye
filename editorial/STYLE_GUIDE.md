# History desk: standards

This is the guide the weekly AI draft is given, and the guide a person checks it against.

## The theme: Panjabi aa gaye oye
The site's name means "the Punjabis have arrived." The history archive tells how, in order: the homeland and
the reasons for leaving, the first arrivals, the fight to stay, the exclusion years, the doors reopening, and
the community that is here today. Every article, whatever its subject, should help a reader see one step of
that journey:
- Say where the story sits in the arrival (who came, who was kept out, who stayed, what they built).
- Prefer stories with a Pacific Northwest or British Columbia thread. Stories from Panjab or from music and
  culture should connect to what people carried with them.
- Give each article a year (the year the story is about). It places the article on the timeline.

## The standard
- Accuracy over drama. A plain true sentence beats a vivid unverified one.
- Every date, number, name and quotation must trace to a source listed at the bottom of the article.
- Never invent a quotation, a statistic or a source. If it cannot be confirmed, leave it out.
- When sources disagree (they often do on casualty counts and dates), give the range and say the sources differ.
- Attribute contested claims ("historian X argues", "the report states"). Do not decide disputes the sources have not settled.
- Say what is tradition and what is documented. Devotional accounts are reported as what the community holds, with attribution.
- No sectarian, triumphalist or hagiographic language. No blaming whole communities.
- Explain terms the first time (gurdwara, Khalsa, Ghadar, langar).

## Sources
- At least three, from at least two kinds: an encyclopedia or university project, a museum or archive, a scholarly article or book, a major news report.
- Prefer institutions and primary records. Wikipedia can help you find leads but should not be the only support for a claim.
- Link to pages that are free to read. Give each source a short readable label.
- Do not copy a source's wording. Write in your own words; quote only a short, attributed phrase.

## Writing
- 550 to 900 words. Short paragraphs. A plain opening that says what happened and where.
- Use "Panjab" for the historical region and "Punjabi" for the people and language, matching the site.
- Dates in the form "September 4, 1907". Years as numerals.
- A local thread (Seattle, Bellingham, Vancouver, Surrey) only where a source supports it.
- End with why it still matters, not with a moral.

## Sensitive topics
Partition, 1984 and violence against communities are written by a person, read by a second person, and never left to the automation (they carry `auto: false` in the calendar). If a draft touches them anyway, treat it as sensitive: two readers, every figure checked, no graphic detail that a source does not need.

## Before publishing (about 15 minutes)
1. Open each listed source and confirm the facts you can. Check every date and number.
2. Read the "Verification notes" section. Resolve or remove each item.
3. Check every direct quotation against its source, or paraphrase it.
4. Read it aloud once. Cut anything you would not say to a survivor's grandchild.
5. Add the `history` label to publish. Leave the "AI assistance" field as it is; the page then tells readers.
